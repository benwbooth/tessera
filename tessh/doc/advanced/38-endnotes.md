The ABS Guide ends with its author's notes. Mendel Cooper wrote it because
the book he wanted to learn from didn't exist: one that took hard ideas,
turned them inside out and explained them with well-commented examples.
He tells of the mad professor who, on seeing any book, rushed home to
write a better one with the same title, and of his own path from FORTRAN
IV on paper coding pads to Linux in 1995. He no longer maintains the
Guide or answers questions about it. It was written in vim and DocBook
SGML, on a series of used ThinkPads, with help from the many contributors
its credits list, and it is in the public domain (see the introduction).

This companion follows the Guide chapter by chapter. Its tessh examples
were run with tessh itself, and the outputs shown are what they printed;
the bash originals were run alongside where they can run here, and their
output is shown where it differs. Where tessh can't do what bash does, the
text says so, in paragraphs marked **Gap:**.

For help with tessh: `help` at the REPL starts the guide, `help TOPIC`
shows one (`help pipes`, `help abs-debugging`), and `help $value` shows
Python's help for a value. `tessh --check` finds mistakes in a script
without running it, and `tessh --py` shows the Python it becomes.

*Doce ut discas*: teach, that you yourself may learn.
